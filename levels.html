CEFR-style curriculum

What you'll be able to do, level by level

Deutschpfad's curriculum is organized around the six levels of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) — A1 through C2. Rather than listing grammar topics, each level is described as a set of things you can actually do in German once you've reached it.

Where things stand: this page describes the full curriculum design. All twelve planned A1 topic units are live — Unit 1: Greetings, Unit 2: Introducing Yourself, Unit 3: Numbers (built first, as the reference lesson for the site's engine), Unit 4: Family, Unit 5: Colors, Unit 6: Days, Months, and Seasons, Unit 7: Time, Unit 8: Food, Unit 9: Drinks, Unit 10: Home, Unit 11: Animals, and Unit 12: Daily Life — and an exam-readiness expansion is now underway, to close real gaps against what a Goethe/telc A1 exam actually expects: Unit 13: Questions & Negation, Unit 14: Pronouns & Cases, Unit 15: Modal Verbs, and Unit 16: Perfekt (Past Tense) — all four grammar units are now done.

Deutschpfad does not issue an official certification. The CEFR level shown on your local dashboard is an estimate of your own progress through this site's material, meant to help you track growth — not a substitute for a recognized language exam.

A1

Absolute beginner

Breakthrough

  • I can introduce myself and give basic personal information (name, age, origin).
  • I can understand and use familiar, everyday expressions and very simple phrases.
  • I can ask and answer simple questions about familiar, concrete topics.
  • I can interact in a simple way if the other person speaks slowly and clearly.

Typical topics: greetings and introductions, numbers, family, colors, days and time, food and drink, the home, animals, and daily routines.

A2

Elementary

Waystage

  • I can describe my daily routine, my surroundings, and my immediate needs.
  • I can communicate in simple, routine situations requiring a direct exchange of information.
  • I can talk about my background, education, and immediate environment in simple terms.
  • I can handle short social exchanges, even if I usually can't sustain a longer conversation.

Typical topics: shopping, directions, public transport, ordering at a café or restaurant, simple past events, and describing people and places.

Start Unit 1: Präteritum → Unit 2: Comparatives → Unit 3: Two-Way Prepositions → Unit 4: Subordinate Clauses → Unit 5: Reflexive Verbs → Unit 6: Dative-only Prepositions → Unit 7: Travel & Holidays → Unit 8: City Life & Getting Around → Unit 9: Health & the Body → Unit 10: The Genitiv →

A2 is in progress — ten units so far, more to come.

B1

Intermediate

Threshold

  • I can explain my experiences, opinions, dreams, and plans, with reasons.
  • I can deal with most situations likely to arise while traveling in a German-speaking area.
  • I can produce simple, connected text on topics that are familiar or of personal interest.
  • I can understand the main points of clear, standard input on familiar matters.

Typical topics: university and work life, renting an apartment, doctor's visits, opinions and arguments, narrating past experiences, and navigating administration as a newcomer.

B2

Upper intermediate

Vantage

  • I can understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics.
  • I can interact with a degree of fluency that makes regular interaction with native speakers possible.
  • I can produce clear, detailed text and explain a viewpoint, weighing advantages and disadvantages.
  • I can follow extended speech and complex lines of argument, provided the topic is reasonably familiar.

Typical topics: academic and professional German, structured argument and debate, media and current events, and nuanced narrative and description.

C1

Advanced

Effective operational proficiency

  • I can understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts and recognize implicit meaning.
  • I can express myself fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions.
  • I can use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes.
  • I can produce well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects.

Typical topics: literature and longer prose, historical and cultural analysis, formal and academic writing, and nuanced argumentation.

C2

Mastery

Full operational command

  • I can understand virtually everything heard or read with ease.
  • I can summarize information from different spoken and written sources, reconstructing arguments coherently.
  • I can express myself spontaneously, very fluently, and precisely in complex situations.
  • I can differentiate finer shades of meaning, even in the most complex texts.

Typical topics: authentic literature, specialized and technical text, and near-native command of register and nuance.
