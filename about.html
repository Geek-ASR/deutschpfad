About the project

Learn German by exploring it, not just drilling it

Deutschpfad is built around one idea: the fastest way to actually learn German is to use it to explore something you're curious about — a story, a piece of history, a city, a real conversation — not to grind through an endless deck of flashcards.

Not another flashcard app

Plenty of language apps reduce learning to "vocabulary → multiple choice → done." Deutschpfad is built differently: vocabulary and grammar are taught in context, then revisited later inside stories, real-life scenarios, German history, and geography, so the same word or pattern keeps resurfacing in new situations instead of being seen once and forgotten.

The goal is to move a learner from "I am studying German" toward "I am using German to explore the German-speaking world."

Who this is for

Deutschpfad is built primarily for international students learning German — for university, for daily life, or simply out of interest — who want a serious, structured course without a subscription, a paywall, or an account. It's free for anyone to use.

Privacy & your data

Deutschpfad has no login and no account system, so there's no user database, no password, and no email address collected — because none of that exists on this site.

Any progress the site remembers — lessons completed, vocabulary you've marked as learned or difficult, quiz scores, streaks — is saved using your browser's built-in local storage, directly on your device. That data is never transmitted anywhere: there's no server for it to be sent to.

What this means in practice: your progress is tied to one browser on one device. Clearing your browser's site data will erase it, and it won't automatically appear if you switch browsers or devices.

To make up for that, the progress dashboard includes export and import: a button to download your progress as a small file, and another to load that file back in — on the same device after clearing data, or on a different one. That gives account-like portability without ever requiring an account.

The only thing Deutschpfad may ever ask of your browser, beyond local storage, is to load its own static files. It does not use third-party analytics, ad trackers, or embedded fonts from external services — even small conveniences like that would mean your browser quietly talking to a server it doesn't need to.

Open source & how it's built

The site is plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no framework, no build step, no bundler. That's a deliberate choice, not a limitation: it keeps the whole project inspectable, fast, and simple to run anywhere, including directly from GitHub Pages with zero server costs, ever.

The code is MIT-licensed and open for anyone to read, run, or reuse. Educational content (lessons, stories, explanations) carries its own license, tracked in the repository, since original writing and public-domain material are treated differently. See the project's LICENSE and CONTENT-LICENSE.md files for specifics.

Deutschpfad is created and maintained by Aditya Rekhe, and built in the open — the full commit history is in the repository, and the curriculum plan tracks what's live and what's next.

Where the project is right now

Deutschpfad is being built in public, in phases: first the design system and site structure, then the lesson and quiz engines, then local progress tracking, then stories, history, and real-life scenarios. See the current curriculum plan for details on what's live and what's next.
