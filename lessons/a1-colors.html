    Step 2 · Understand

    Why colors sometimes change and sometimes don't

    In English, red is always just red — "the apple is red," "the red apple," no difference. German color words can change form depending on where they sit in the sentence:

    After ist or sind ("is"/"are"), a color word stays in its plain, dictionary form — Der Apfel ist rot. Directly in front of a noun, it takes an ending — der rote Apfel. That ending depends on the noun's gender and grammatical case, which is a full topic of its own — you'll meet it properly in a later unit. For now, this lesson sticks to the ist/sind form, which is what you'll reach for most as a beginner.

    Three colors — rosa, orange, and lila — are an exception worth knowing: they never take an ending at all, even in front of a noun. One less thing to remember for those three.
