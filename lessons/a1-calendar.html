Days, Months, and Seasons

Goal: I can name the days of the week, months, and seasons, and use am or im correctly to say when something happens.

    Step 2 · Understand

    Two reliable shortcuts

    This unit has more new words than most — seven days, twelve months, four seasons — but two things make them easier than they look.

    First: every single one of them is der. Most German nouns need their gender memorized individually, but days, months, and seasons are one of the few genuinely reliable patterns — der Montag, der Juni, der Winter, no exceptions.

    Second: saying when something happens uses a short fused word instead of a separate preposition — am (from an dem) for days, and im (from in dem) for months and seasons: am Montag, im Juni, im Winter.

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      am and im are your first fused prepositions — short forms that combine a preposition with a definite article. You'll meet more of these (zum, zur, ins, beim) as the curriculum continues; the logic is always the same fusion, just with different pieces.
