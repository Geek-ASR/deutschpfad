A2 · Unit 19

The n-Declension

Goal: I know the small group of masculine nouns that add -n / -en in every case but the nominative singular — der Student → den Studenten, der Herr → den Herrn.

Step 1 · Discover

The extra -en

Before any rules — the same noun as a subject, then as an object.

    Step 2 · Understand

    A small group that changes everywhere

    Most masculine nouns only add something in the genitive (des Mannes, Unit 10) and stay put in the accusative and dative. A small group — the n-nouns (or "weak nouns") — adds -n or -en in every case except the nominative singular.

    Which nouns. Masculine person / animal words ending in -e: der Junge, der Kunde, der Kollege, der Experte, der Löwe, der Affe. International "person" words ending in -ent, -ant, -ist, -at: der Student, der Praktikant, der Polizist, der Journalist, der Tourist, der Kandidat, der Diplomat. And a short memorised set: der Herr, der Mensch, der Nachbar, der Bauer, der Held, der Name.

    singular plural
    Nomder Kollegedie Kollegen
    Akkden Kollegendie Kollegen
    Datdem Kollegenden Kollegen
    Gendes Kollegender Kollegen

    Only der Kollege — the nominative singular — is bare. Everything else is Kollegen.

    Two irregulars. der Herr → den / dem / des Herrn in the singular, but die Herren in the plural. der Name → den / dem Namen, but the genitive adds an extra -s: des Namens. Same for der Gedanke, der Wille, der Friede.

    Where the mistake happens. As a direct object or after a preposition: Ich kenne den Studenten (not den Student), Ich wohne bei einem Kollegen, mit meinem Nachbarn.

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      These words are common — Kollege, Kunde, Nachbar, Herr, Mensch come up in every conversation about people. Once the -en is automatic you stop making the one mistake that instantly marks a sentence as learner German. B1 just expects it to be right without thinking.
