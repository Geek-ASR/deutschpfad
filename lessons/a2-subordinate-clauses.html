    Step 2 · Understand

    One conjunction, and the verb goes to the end

    A subordinate clause can't stand alone — it adds a reason, a condition, or a reported thought to a main clause. In German, the conjunction that starts it (weil, dass, wenn, ob, obwohl) kicks the conjugated verb all the way to the end: Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil es regnet ("...because it's raining"). There's always a comma before the conjunction.

    If the subordinate clause comes first, the main clause has to start with its verb — the two verbs end up next to each other, "kissing" across the comma: Wenn es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause.

    Two traps. denn also means "because" but is a coordinating conjunction — the word order after it doesn't change at all: ..., denn es regnet (verb still in position 2). And deshalb ("therefore") isn't a conjunction — it's an adverb that takes position 1, so the verb comes second: Es regnet, deshalb bleibe ich zu Hause.

    wenn vs. als. Use wenn for something repeated or possible ("whenever it rains", "if it rains"). Use als — from A2 Unit 1 — for a single completed event in the past: Als ich klein war, ....

      Where this shows up next
