Adjective Endings after ein/kein/mein

Goal: I can put the right ending on an adjective after ein, kein or a possessive — ein alter Mann, ein kleines Kind, meine alten Schuhe.

Step 1 · Discover

One letter's difference

Before any rules — the Unit 12 phrase, then the same thing with ein. Watch the adjective ending.

    Step 2 · Understand

    Weak, plus three fill-ins

    Same starting point as Unit 12: predicate adjectives don't change; attributive ones do. After ein / kein / mein … the pattern is called mixed — it's the weak endings you already know, with three exceptions.

    The three exceptions. ein has no ending at all in exactly three spots: nominative masculine (ein Mann), nominative neuter and accusative neuter (ein Kind). In those three, the adjective has to show the gender the article isn't showing, so it takes the strong ending — -er for masculine (ein alter Mann) and -es for neuter (ein altes Kind).

    Everywhere else = Unit 12. eine, einen, einem, einer all carry an ending already, so the adjective is weak: -e in eine gute Frau (nom/acc feminine), -en in every dative, every genitive, the masculine accusative (einen guten Mann), and the whole plural.

    m f n pl (mein…)
    Nomein altereine alteein altesmeine alten
    Akkeinen alteneine alteein altesmeine alten
    Dateinem alteneiner alteneinem altenmeinen alten
    Geneines alteneiner alteneines altenmeiner alten

    The three bold non--en cells outside the feminine column are the whole difference from Unit 12.

    kein and the possessives (mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr) decline exactly like ein — and unlike ein they have a plural, where the adjective is always -en. Two spelling notes: dunkel and teuer drop the -e- before an ending (ein dunkles Zimmer, ein teures Auto).

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      You now have two of the three adjective patterns. The last — no article at all (guter Kaffee, kaltes Wasser) — just moves every article ending straight onto the adjective; it's a short step from here. All three come up constantly in the B1 writing tasks, where "describe your flat / your town / your ideal job" is a standard prompt.
