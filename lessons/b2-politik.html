    Step 2 · Understand

    Germany-specific vocabulary

    Some of this unit is generic political vocabulary; some of it only makes sense once you know how Germany's system actually works.

    Wählen vs. abstimmen. B1's wählen elects a person or party; this unit's abstimmen votes on a specific issue or bill, usually inside a body like the Bundestag.

    Grundgesetz, not Verfassung. Germany's constitution is called das Grundgesetz — "Basic Law" — for historical reasons dating to 1949, not the generic word die Verfassung.

    The Bundeskanzler isn't directly elected. Voters elect the Bundestag; the Bundestag then elects the Bundeskanzler — a common point of confusion for learners used to directly-elected heads of government.

    Coalitions are the norm, not the exception. Germany's multi-party system means a single party almost never wins an outright majority, so die Koalition is one of the most important words in this unit.
