    Step 2 · Understand

    Verbs that come apart on purpose

    Many German verbs are built from a small prefix plus a base verb — auf + stehen = aufstehen ("to get up"). In the dictionary and as an infinitive, they stay together. But conjugate one for ich/du/etc. in a main clause, and the prefix detaches and jumps to the very end of the sentence:

    aufstehen → Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf. Not Ich aufstehe um sieben Uhr — the conjugated piece (stehe) stays in its normal position, and the prefix (auf) goes all the way to the end, no matter how long the sentence gets in between.

    One trap: frühstücken ("to have breakfast") looks like it should split the same way — but it doesn't. It's one word straight through: Ich frühstücke um acht Uhr, never Ich früh...stücke. Looking separable and being separable aren't the same thing — the picker below is a reliable way to check.
