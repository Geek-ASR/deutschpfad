    Step 2 · Understand

    Three word-order patterns, and two ways to say no

    German sentences follow one of three shapes depending on what they're doing. A statement puts the verb second: Du kommst mit. A yes/no question puts the verb first: Kommst du mit? A question with a question word puts the word first, then the verb second: Wann kommst du? Same verb, same subject — only the order changes, and it changes what kind of sentence it is.

    Two ways to say "not": kein negates a noun, and it changes form exactly the way ein did in the Drinks unit — einen Kaffee becomes keinen Kaffee, eine Cola becomes keine Cola, ein Wasser becomes kein Wasser. nicht negates everything else — verbs, adjectives, whole statements: Der Kaffee ist nicht heiß.

    One more useful word: if someone asks a negative question and the true answer is actually "yes," German has a special word for that instead of plain ja — doch. Hast du keinen Hunger? ("Aren't you hungry?") — if you actually are, the answer is Doch!, not Ja.
