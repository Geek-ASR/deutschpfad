Funktionsverbgefüge

Goal: I can recognize and use the formal "light verb + noun" constructions German uses instead of a single plain verb, and unpack them when reading.

Step 1 · Discover

Two phrases that each mean one simple verb

The kind of language a law, a contract, or a news article actually uses.

    Step 2 · Understand

    A "light" verb plus a noun

    A Funktionsverbgefüge pairs a near-meaningless verb (nehmen, stellen, bringen, treten…) with a noun to express what a single verb could say more plainly — and it's everywhere in formal German.

    The nehmen-family. Rücksicht nehmen auf, in Anspruch nehmen, Einfluss nehmen auf, Kontakt aufnehmen mit, Abstand nehmen von — five very common phrases built on the same light verb.

    The stellen-family, and treten/setzen. zur Verfügung stellen, in Frage stellen, unter Beweis stellen — and the opposite pair in Kraft treten / außer Kraft setzen for a law starting or stopping.

    The bringen-family, and a few standalones. zum Ausdruck bringen (a feeling), zur Sprache bringen (a topic) — plus Kritik üben an, eine Entscheidung treffen, in Betracht ziehen, and Anwendung finden.

    The reading skill: unpacking to one verb. Every Funktionsverbgefüge here has a plain-verb equivalent — Rücksicht nehmen auf = berücksichtigen, Kritik üben an = kritisieren — the same unpacking skill as B1 Unit 10's Verbalisierung.

    Where you'll actually see this. Laws, contracts, official notices, and news articles reach for a Funktionsverbgefüge far more often than casual speech does — recognizing them is a reading skill as much as a speaking one.
