A2 · Unit 6

Dative-only Prepositions

Goal: I can use mit, nach, zu, von, bei, seit, aus, gegenüber — which always take the Dativ — and the fused forms zum, zur, vom, beim.

Step 1 · Discover

No choice to make

Before any rules — two real sentences. Both prepositions are followed by the Dativ, no matter what the sentence is doing.

    Step 2 · Understand

    These eight are always Dativ

    The two-way prepositions in Unit 3 made you choose — Dativ for a location, Akkusativ for movement. This set is easier in one way: there's no choice. mit, nach, zu, von, bei, seit, aus, gegenüber take the Dativ every time, whether something is sitting still or moving.

    Fused forms — these are not optional. zu dem → zum, zu der → zur, von dem → vom, bei dem → beim. You'll almost never hear the long form: it's zum Bahnhof, zur Post, vom Arzt, beim Zahnarzt.

    nach or zu for "to"? Use nach with cities and most countries (no article): nach Berlin, nach Italien — plus the fixed phrase nach Hause ("homewards"). Use zu for people and for a place you're heading to: zu mir, zum Arzt, zur Schule. "At home" (no movement) is its own phrase: zu Hause.

    aus vs. von, and seit. aus = out of an enclosed space, or where you're from (Ich komme aus der Türkei), or what a thing is made of. von = from a person or a point you're leaving (ein Brief von meinem Bruder, vom Bahnhof). seit marks something that started in the past and is still going — and German keeps it in the present tense: Ich lerne seit einem Jahr Deutsch ("I've been learning German for a year").

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      These prepositions are unavoidable — every route (mit dem Bus zum Bahnhof), every introduction (Ich komme aus …, Ich wohne bei …), every "how long" answer (seit …). The travel and city-life units ahead lean on them constantly, and seit + present tense comes straight back when you talk about how long you've done something.
