Indirect Speech — Konjunktiv I

Goal: I can report what someone said with er sei, er komme, er habe, switch to Konjunktiv II when the forms collide with the indicative, and recognise this register in the news.

    Step 2 · Understand

    One new form, one crucial substitution rule

    Konjunktiv I is a whole new set of endings — but you only really need to trust one part of it.

    Konjunktiv I: stem + endings. kommen → er komme, sein → er sei (irregular), haben → er habe. Built from the infinitive stem, so no umlaut surprises.

    The rule that matters: avoid collisions. When Konjunktiv I looks exactly like the indicative (ich/wir/sie-forms usually do — sie kommen either way), German substitutes Konjunktiv II instead: sie sagten, sie kämen später, not *sie kommen.

    Two ways to say it. dass + verb-final (Er sagte, dass er müde sei), or drop dass and keep the verb second (Er sagte, er sei müde). Same meaning; the second is more typical of news writing.

    One past form for every original tense. habe/sei (Konjunktiv I) + Partizip II covers Präteritum, Perfekt, and Plusquamperfekt in the original: "Ich war müde." → Er sagte, er sei müde gewesen.

    Questions and commands. ob reports a yes/no question (Sie fragte, ob ich käme); a W-word carries over unchanged (Er fragte, wann ich käme); sollen reports a command (Er sagte, ich solle das machen).

    Mostly a written register. In everyday speech, people say "Er hat gesagt, dass er müde ist" or use soll/will (Unit 8) instead. Konjunktiv I is a reading skill first — you'll meet it constantly in the news.

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      Konjunktiv I is everywhere in written news and official statements — recognising it fluently is a core B1 reading skill. It builds directly on the Konjunktiv II of Unit 1 and the past-tense logic of Unit 5, and it's the last of the B1 grammar backbone before the topic units.
