Futur I & II

Goal: I can make a genuine prediction or promise with werden + Infinitiv, and I can recognize when Futur I or II is actually just a guess about the present or the past.

    Step 2 · Understand

    Two tenses, and what they're actually for

    You've used werden as a full verb since A1. This unit is about it as an auxiliary.

    Futur I — the form. werden conjugated in position 2, the full verb's infinitive at the very end: Ich werde das erledigen. But for a planned, scheduled event, spoken German almost always just uses the present tense instead: Ich fahre morgen nach Berlin.

    So what is Futur I actually for? Two real jobs: a genuine prediction about the more distant future (In zehn Jahren werden die meisten Autos elektrisch fahren.), or a firm promise (Ich werde dir helfen, das verspreche ich.).

    The trap: Futur I as a guess about now. Add wohl, wahrscheinlich, or vermutlich, and Futur I stops being about the future at all — it becomes an assumption about the present: Er wird jetzt wohl noch in der Besprechung sein. = "He's probably still in the meeting" — right now, not later.

    Futur II — the form. werden + Partizip II + haben/sein, all stacked at the end: Sie wird den Bus verpasst haben. Its most common real job is a guess about the past — "she's probably missed the bus" — not a future-perfect statement at all.

    Futur II's one genuinely future use. Paired with a deadline, it means "will have done X by Y": Bis Freitag werde ich den Bericht fertiggestellt haben. And the Konjunktiv II modal dürfte + Infinitiv (Unit 8, B1) says almost the same thing as a Futur II guess: Das dürfte stimmen.
