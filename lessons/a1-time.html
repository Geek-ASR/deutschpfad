Time

    Step 2 · Understand

    Two systems, and one real trap

    German has two ways to tell time. The formal style just says the numbers — vierzehn Uhr dreißig for 14:30 — the same 24-hour numbers you already know from the Numbers unit. It's unambiguous and used on schedules, tickets, and the news. The everyday spoken style is what people actually say in conversation, and it's built from nach (past), vor (to), and Viertel (quarter) — much like English "quarter past nine" or "quarter to ten."

    The one real trap: halb ("half") doesn't work like English. halb neun does not mean 9:30 — it means 8:30, because it's "half [of the way] to nine," counting toward the next hour rather than past the current one. Read it as "halfway to nine," not "half past nine," and it stops being confusing.
