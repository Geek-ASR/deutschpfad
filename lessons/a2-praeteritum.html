    Step 2 · Understand

    One past for speaking, one for the page

    In A1 you learned the Perfekt (Ich habe gegessen) — that's the past tense Germans say. The Präteritum is the one Germans write: news, stories, reports, anything narrated. Reading German at A2 means meeting it constantly, so the first job is just recognizing it.

    But two groups of verbs use the Präteritum in speech too, and you'll want these actively: sein → war, haben → hatte, and the modal verbs — können → konnte, wollen → wollte, müssen → musste. Nobody says "Ich habe gemusst" in conversation; they say "Ich musste".

    Forming it splits the same way the Perfekt participle did. Weak verbs add -te to the stem (machen → machte, sagen → sagte) — predictable. Strong verbs change the stem vowel and add nothing in the ich/er form (gehen → ging, kommen → kam, geben → gab) — memorized per verb, like English "go → went". sein, haben, and werden are just irregular: war, hatte, wurde. Endings after that are the usual ones — du adds -st, wir/sie add -(e)n.

      Where this shows up next
