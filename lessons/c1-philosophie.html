Philosophy & Abstract Argument

Goal: I can name philosophy's major branches, describe the pieces of a formal argument, and talk about a handful of famous positions — determinism, utilitarianism, the categorical imperative — with precision.

    Step 2 · Understand

    Branches, arguments, positions

    This unit has three layers: the traditional branches of philosophy, the vocabulary for describing how a formal argument is built, and a set of well-known philosophical positions.

    Different branches ask different questions. Metaphysik asks what's real; Erkenntnistheorie asks what we can know; Ethik asks what's right.

    Arguments are built from premises. A Prämisse supports a Schlussfolgerung — put two together correctly and you have a Syllogismus.

    Famous positions have names. Determinismus vs. freier Wille; Utilitarismus vs. Kant's kategorischer Imperativ.

    Objectivity and subjectivity cut across all of it. Is a claim objektiv or subjektiv — and is a single, universal Wahrheit even possible?
