Step 1 · Discover

Two sentences that open with a whole scene

    Step 2 · Understand

    Not the same as B1's participles

    B1 Unit 9's participles sat before a noun (der schnell fahrende Zug). These sit at the front of a whole sentence instead, doing the job of a subordinate clause.

    Partizip I: at the same time. Lächelnd verließ sie den Raum. — describes what the subject is doing at the same moment as the main action, like a compressed während-clause.

    Partizip II: passive, or already finished. Von allen bewundert, genoss er seinen Erfolg. (passive — something done to him) or In Berlin angekommen, suchte sie ein Hotel. (finished before the main action, for sein-verbs).

    The one rule that always holds. Whoever or whatever the participle describes must be the same as the main clause's subject. Auf die Frage angesprochen, antwortete sie ruhig. — the person asked and the person answering are the same sie.

    Comma, always. Whether the phrase opens or closes the sentence, a comma marks the boundary — the same way it would around a subordinate clause.

    Unpacking it. Depending on the meaning, you can rewrite the phrase as nachdem (completed before), weil (a reason), or während (simultaneous) — context tells you which.
