    Step 2 · Understand

    Why German numbers sound "backwards"

    English builds numbers 21–99 tens-first: twenty-one. German builds them ones-first: einundzwanzig — literally "one-and-twenty." It's the same logic English used to use too (older forms like "four-and-twenty" survive in nursery rhymes) — German just kept it as the standard pattern for every number from 21 to 99.

    ein (one) + und (and) + zwanzig (twenty) = einundzwanzig (twenty-one)

    That's really the whole rule for 21–99. The next step lets you test it on any number.

    Ordinal numbers — "1st, 2nd, 3rd..."

    A different, smaller rule: add -te below twenty, -ste from twenty on — except four irregular stems, the same way English has "first" and "third" instead of "oneth" and "threeth." This is the base form; like any adjective, it takes a different ending depending on gender and case (dates in particular use am ersten, not der erste) — a full topic of its own for a later unit.
