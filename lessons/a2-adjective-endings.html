A2 · Unit 12

Adjective Endings after der/die/das

Goal: I can put the right ending on an adjective that stands between the definite article and its noun — der alte Mann, den alten Mann, mit den alten Männern.

Step 1 · Discover

The same adjective, twice

Before any rules — two sentences with alt. It only grows an ending in one of them.

    Step 2 · Understand

    Only before a noun, and only two endings

    An adjective after sein or werden — a predicate adjective — never changes: Der Mann ist alt. This unit is about the other position: an attributive adjective, squeezed between an article and its noun.

    After der/die/das, there are only two possible endings: -e and -en. That's why this is called the weak declension — the article has already done the work of showing case and gender, so the adjective barely moves. This is the easiest of the three adjective patterns.

    -e in five slots: the nominative singular of all three genders (der gute, die gute, das gute) and the accusative singular of die and das (die gute, das gute). -en everywhere else: the masculine accusative (den guten), every dative, every genitive (Unit 10), and the entire plural.

    m f n pl
    Nomder altedie altedas altedie alten
    Akkden altendie altedas altedie alten
    Datdem altender altendem altenden alten
    Gendes altender altendes altender alten

    Same endings after the der-words: dieser, jeder, welcher, mancher, alle. And comparatives and superlatives decline too: der kleinere Bruder, das beste Ergebnis (Unit 2). A few words never change: rosa, lila, prima, and adverb-like ganz in ganz gut.

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      This is the first of three adjective patterns. Next comes the ein-word set (ein alter Mann — the adjective has to show the gender the article can't), then the no-article set. Get the weak one automatic and the other two are just "fill in what der/die/das would have shown".
