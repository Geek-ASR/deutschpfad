Konjunktiv I: Modal Verbs & Reporting the Future

Goal: I can report what someone said using any of the six modal verbs, report what someone said they'd had to do or couldn't do, and report a claim about something being finished by some future point.

    Step 2 · Understand

    Modals in the Konjunktiv I system

    B1 Unit 11 gave you sein, haben, and werden in Konjunktiv I. This unit fills in the six modal verbs, plus two things that only come up once a modal is involved.

    The six modals, singular forms. ich könne, du könnest, er könne — and the same pattern for müssen, dürfen, wollen, sollen, mögen. Unlike most verbs, these singular forms rarely collide with the indicative, so you'll meet them constantly in formal and journalistic German.

    The plural Ersatzform, again. wir können / sie können (Konjunktiv I) still collide with the indicative, so wir könnten / sie könnten (Konjunktiv II) substitutes — the same rule from B1 Unit 11, just applied to modals.

    The reported past with a modal: a double infinitive. Sie sagte, sie habe nicht kommen können. — a modal combined with another verb never uses a participle like gekonnt in the Perfekt, so reported speech keeps that double infinitive too, pushed to the very end of the clause.

    Reporting a completed future: Futur II. Er behauptete, er werde das Projekt bis Freitag abgeschlossen haben. — deepens B2 Unit 1's Futur II by using it to report someone's own prediction about a future-finished action.

    Not the same as your own guess. Konjunktiv I distances you from someone else's claim. B1 Unit 8's dürfte / müsste voice your own guess instead — same neutral tone, different source.
