Modal Verbs

Goal: I can use können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, sollen, and mögen, putting the main verb's infinitive at the end of the sentence.

    Step 2 · Understand

    A verb you already half-know, and a familiar word order

    You met möchten ("would like to") back in the Drinks unit without calling it a modal verb. It is one — and German has five more: können (can), müssen (must), wollen (want to), dürfen (may), and sollen (should). They're all irregular the same way: the ich-form and the er/sie/es-form are always identical, with no ending at all — ich kann, er kann, never kanne or kannt.

    The word order should look familiar too. In the Daily Life unit, separable verbs sent their prefix all the way to the end of the sentence. Modal verbs do the same thing, but with a whole second verb: the modal takes the normal conjugated spot, and the infinitive of the main verb goes to the very end — Ich kann gut Deutsch sprechen ("I can speak German well"), no matter how much sits in between.

    One exception each: sollen is the only one of the six that doesn't change its stem vowel (ich soll, not ich sölle). And mögen usually doesn't pair with a second verb at all — Ich mag Pizza stands on its own, the same way English "I like pizza" doesn't need a "to."
