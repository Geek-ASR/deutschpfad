Asking, and answering

Before any rules — someone asks the way, someone answers. Notice how the answer's verbs come first.

    Step 2 · Understand

    The imperative — telling someone what to do

    Directions are built almost entirely out of one form you haven't had a unit on yet: the imperative. It has three versions, one per "you".

    Sie — verb first, then Sie. It looks exactly like a yes/no question: Gehen Sie geradeaus. Nehmen Sie die erste Straße rechts. Add bitte to soften: Nehmen Sie bitte die Treppe.

    du — the bare stem, no ending, no pronoun. du gehst → Geh!, du wartest → Warte! (keep the -e when the stem ends in -t or -d). Stem-changing e → i/ie verbs keep the change: nehmen → Nimm!, geben → Gib! — but a → ä verbs drop the umlaut: fahren → Fahr!. The ihr form is just the normal ihr form minus the pronoun: Geht!, Nehmt!

    Separable verbs split. The prefix goes to the end, imperative included: Biegen Sie an der Ampel links ab. Steig an der nächsten Haltestelle aus. (Same verbs as the travel unit.)

    Asking politely = an indirect question. Instead of Wo ist der Bahnhof?, wrap it: Können Sie mir sagen, wo der Bahnhof ist? — the verb goes to the end, just like after weil in Unit 4. And floors and streets use ordinals from A1 Numbers: im ersten Stock, die zweite Straße, in der dritten Etage.

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      The imperative is everywhere outside directions too — recipes (Schneiden Sie die Zwiebeln), advice (Ruh dich aus!), signs (Bitte drücken). The health unit uses it for a doctor's advice, and the indirect question you built here is the same structure behind reported speech later.
