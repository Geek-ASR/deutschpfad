    Step 2 · Understand

    A pronoun that points back at the subject

    Some verbs describe an action you do to yourself, and German marks that with a reflexive pronoun: Ich wasche mich ("I wash myself"). It usually sits right after the conjugated verb. The set is almost the same as the normal object pronouns — only the 3rd person and formal Sie use the special word sich:

    ich → mich, du → dich, er/sie/es → sich, wir → uns, ihr → euch, sie/Sie → sich.

    Accusative or dative? Normally the reflexive pronoun is the object, so it's accusative (mich, dich…). But if the sentence already has a direct object — usually a body part with its article — the reflexive pronoun steps back to the dative (mir, dir…): Ich wasche mir die Hände, Ich putze mir die Zähne.

    Some verbs are "truly reflexive" — they only exist with the pronoun. sich freuen, sich beeilen, sich erinnern: there's no *freuen on its own. Others (like waschen, setzen) can be used with any object; adding the reflexive pronoun just means "to/for myself".

      Step 9 · Encounter again

      Where this shows up next

      Reflexives are everywhere in describing a day — sich anziehen, sich duschen, sich fertig machen — and in feelings and plans: Ich freue mich, dass du kommst. The dative/accusative choice you practised here is the same one behind two-way prepositions and indirect objects.
